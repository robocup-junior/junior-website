## Background

More than twenty five years ago, the birth of personal computers spawned a new era in the age of technology, and educational applications began to infiltrate classrooms. RoboCupJunior draws on two ideas from that time.

{{< about-grid cols="2" >}}
{{< about-card title="Seymour Papert: constructionism" >}}
Papert linked technology with Jean Piaget’s constructivist theory of education to produce LOGO (or “turtle geometry”), a simple programming language that allows young students to learn geometry through computer-based exploration.

He published these ideas in his book *Mindstorms* and introduced the notion of *constructionism*: children learn best when they are actively involved in building something that is meaningful to themselves.
{{< /about-card >}}
{{< about-card title="Howard Gardner: multiple intelligences" >}}
Around the same time, Gardner put forth his “theory of multiple intelligences”, which describes each human mind as a unique combination of talents expressed across a wide range of cognitive spheres.

His book [Frames of Mind](http://www.pz.harvard.edu/resources/frames-of-mind-the-theory-of-multiple-intelligences "newtab") has been extremely influential in education. It has helped drive the trend in classrooms towards teamwork and projects that encourage and motivate different children with different needs.
{{< /about-card >}}
{{< /about-grid >}}

<p class="history-callout">RoboCupJunior aims at bringing together many of these ideas, promoting project-oriented, team-based education, giving children with a variety of interests and abilities an opportunity to pick their own challenges while contributing to the progress of the whole.</p>

## RoboCupJunior International, year by year

### The beginnings: 1998 to 2001

{{< about-timeline >}}
{{< about-year year="1998" place="Paris, France" >}}
RoboCupJunior began with a demonstration at RoboCup-98 in Paris.
{{< /about-year >}}
{{< about-year year="1999" place="Stockholm, Sweden" url="https://web.archive.org/web/20041227163300/http://www.daimi.au.dk:80/~hhl/RoboCupJr/RoboCupJr_report.html" link="Report on the RoboCupJunior workshops (archived)" >}}
RoboCup-99 in Stockholm exhibited the first interactive RoboCupJunior workshops.
{{< /about-year >}}
{{< about-year year="2000" place="Melbourne, Australia" url="http://rcj.sci.brooklyn.cuny.edu/rcj2k/" >}}
Over 100 children participated from 25 schools around Australia, as well as from Germany and the USA. Students entered one of three challenges: soccer, line-following sumo, and dance. In addition, over 500 local school children toured the RoboCup venue and learned about the initiative.
{{< /about-year >}}
{{< about-year year="2001" place="Seattle, USA" teams="25" regions="4" url="http://rcj.sci.brooklyn.cuny.edu/rcj2001/" >}}
Teams from the USA, Australia, Germany and the UK entered soccer, rescue, and OnStage challenges. A workshop followed the tournament, where researchers shared their ideas and progress in educational robotics.
{{< /about-year >}}
