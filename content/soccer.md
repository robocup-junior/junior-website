{{< league-intro tagline="Where autonomous robots compete on the field" >}}
RoboCupJunior Soccer challenges teams of high school students to design, build and program autonomous robots that compete in a fast-paced soccer environment. The robots must navigate the field, track the ball and plan their movements, all without human intervention.

It is an exciting platform for young engineers to develop skills in mechanical design, electrical engineering and artificial intelligence.
{{< /league-intro >}}

{{< league-facts label="Soccer at a glance" >}}
{{< league-fact label="Who" >}}Teams of high school students{{< /league-fact >}}
{{< league-fact label="Main leagues" >}}**2:2 Vision** and **2:2 Infrared**: two robots per team{{< /league-fact >}}
{{< league-fact label="Entry leagues" >}}**1:1** leagues with one robot per team, in select regions and at SuperRegional Tournaments{{< /league-fact >}}
{{< league-fact label="Simulation" >}}**Soccer Simulation Challenge**: fully online, no hardware needed{{< /league-fact >}}
{{< /league-facts >}}

{{< league-section title="2:2 Soccer leagues" lead="The main competitions. Two robots per team face off in dynamic matches that emphasise teamwork, sensor integration and artificial intelligence." >}}
{{< league-grid cols="2" >}}
{{< league-item title="Vision League" image="/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/JuniorCup-1024x683.jpg" alt="Two Soccer robots chasing an orange ball on a green field" >}}
Robots must detect and track an orange golf ball using cameras, which makes this a much greater software challenge. Teams focus on advanced image processing and AI-based movement strategies, so software development is critical to success.
{{< /league-item >}}
{{< league-item title="Infrared League" image="/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/27960913806_9755b1cc96_z.jpg" alt="Students placing their Infrared Soccer robots on the field" >}}
Robots have stricter weight limits but larger size allowances. They track an infrared-emitting ball, relying less on camera-based vision. This league emphasises hardware design and efficient electronic and mechanical engineering.
{{< /league-item >}}

{{< league-section title="1:1 Soccer entry leagues" >}}
{{< league-media image="/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/53674413452_61f2824229_o-1-1024x683.jpg" alt="Entry league Soccer robots on a turquoise field" >}}
For newcomers, the **1:1 Entry Leagues** are an accessible starting point. Each team has one robot, so students can develop foundational skills before moving up to the 2:2 leagues.

<ul class="lg-list">
<li><strong>Soccer 1:1 Standard Kit League:</strong> an introductory league that restricts components, so new participants compete on a level playing field.</li>
<li><strong>Soccer 1:1 Infrared League:</strong> a stepping stone to the 2:2 Lightweight league, with more design flexibility while keeping the focus on fundamentals.</li>
</ul>

Entry leagues run in select regions and at the **SuperRegional Tournaments**. Availability varies, so check with your **regional representative** to confirm what you can enter.

{{< league-section title="Soccer Simulation Challenge" >}}
{{< league-media image="/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/SoccerSim-1024x601.png" alt="Screenshot of the Soccer Simulation: virtual robots on a green field with a GOAL! message" >}}
The **RCJ Soccer Simulation Challenge** is a fully online competition, first introduced at RoboCup Worldwide 2021. Teams submit their code, which is tested on virtual robots on a simulated soccer field.

It is a chance to develop advanced AI and control algorithms without physical hardware, and an excellent entry point for students interested in software-driven robotics.

The competition is not currently running at the International level, but teams may still find it useful for developing their robots.
