What is RoboCupJunior?

RoboCupJunior is a global robotics challenge for school students up to age 19. Teams design, build and program fully autonomous robots, exploring robotics, coding, engineering, teamwork and innovation through hands-on competition.

Students can compete with any robot, any software and any level of experience, from first-time builders to advanced teams preparing for international competition.

Three leagues to choose from

Every league has entry-level divisions for teams taking part for the first time, and a pathway all the way to the RoboCup Major Leagues.

Soccer

Small, autonomous robots play two-on-two games, finding the ball with infrared sensors or cameras and working together as a team.

Rescue

Robots simulate disaster rescues, from line following to exploring a maze, finding and rescuing “victims” in increasingly realistic scenarios.

OnStage

Robots perform creative routines with music, costumes and storytelling, showcasing programming, creativity and engineering.

Why RoboCupJunior?

Built so that any school, club or community group can take part, and keep growing year after year.

Any platform

RoboCupJunior is not tied to one robot kit, brand or programming language. Students build, code and innovate with the tools that work for them.

Consistent global challenges

Internationally recognised challenges support progression from local events through to regional, SuperRegional and international competition.

Skills for the future

Students develop practical STEM skills alongside communication, collaboration, creativity, problem solving and leadership.

A pathway for every student

For over 25 years, RoboCupJunior has guided students from their first steps in robotics to sophisticated projects at local, regional and international events.

It is the educational pathway into RoboCup: many students go on to university RoboCup teams, research and engineering careers, and come back as mentors, judges and organizers.

The RoboCup Objective

RoboCup is an international initiative that has advanced robotics and AI since 1997. Its grand challenge: by 2050, a team of fully autonomous humanoid robots will beat the human World Cup champions.
